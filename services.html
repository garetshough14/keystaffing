Pricing Clarity

What clients are actually paying for.

This page does not hide behind vague language. Exact pricing depends on role complexity, urgency, shift, risk, and scope, but the model should still be easy to understand.

Temporary Staffing

Bill rate is more than pay rate.

Pay Rate + Employer Burden + Recruiting, Payroll, Admin + Risk and Accountability

That is where workers' comp, payroll taxes, recruiting effort, replacement risk, and service delivery live.

Direct Hire Recruiting

Usually scoped as a placement fee.

Pricing is typically tied to compensation level, search difficulty, and urgency. Harder, higher-stakes roles require more market mapping, candidate qualification, and stakeholder calibration.

RPO / Embedded Support

Usually monthly, project-based, or capacity-based.

This covers recruiter capacity, workflow ownership, reporting, stakeholder coordination, and process design for companies that need a hiring engine, not a one-off search.

Consulting & Leadership

Scoped to the business problem.

Consulting work is usually priced around the complexity of the issue, the timeline, the people involved, and the level of hands-on implementation required.

Why Cheap Gets Expensive

Low rates can hide high operating costs.

Cheap staffing can feel like a win until turnover, missed production, retraining time, safety exposure, and supervisor burnout start landing back on your balance sheet.

Missed fit costs money

Every bad match drains time from operations, HR, onboarding, and leadership attention.

Reactive hiring compounds chaos

When intake is weak and the need is urgent, bad process becomes expensive process.

Premium can be operationally cheaper

Higher standards, better communication, and better fit often cost less than constant replacement.

Pricing Philosophy

Cheap staffing is usually not cheaper. It just hides the real bill until turnover, retraining, and missed output show up somewhere else.

The goal here is to make the investment easier to understand, easier to compare, and harder to underestimate.
